The same set of documents can yield two quite different accounts of what an election was about. I wanted to know how much of that difference came from the choice of topic model. On this corpus, non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) returned a more varied set of top words than latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA).

Research Question#

How consistently can an unsupervised model distinguish recurring policy themes in the English-language manifestos of four parties in the 2024 Indian election? I compared two topic models on a common paragraph-level corpus. The evaluation asked three related questions: whether their top words differed across topics, whether those words tended to appear in compatible contexts, and whether comparable topics reappeared in repeated fits.

One motivation for studying the documents together is that parties can discuss the same issue in different terms. A model based on recurring words may group passages by policy area, party vocabulary, or features of the documents themselves. Discovering that a group of words is distinctive is only the first step towards deciding which of those interpretations is justified.

Dataset and Paragraph Construction#

The source collection contains the English manifestos of BJP, Congress, CPI(M), and TMC for one election. Paragraphs are the units supplied to the models. Treating a paragraph as an observation retains more local context than counting words across a whole party document. It also means that observations from one document can resemble each other because of repeated headings, campaign phrasing, or references to earlier sections.

The balanced corpus contains 832 paragraphs, 208 per party. Balancing limits the influence of a longer document on the fitted topics. It also changes the question a reader can answer with those topics. A party’s proportion in the balanced sample is fixed by design. It cannot be used to estimate how much text the party devoted to an issue in the complete manifesto. For that question I would return to the full extracted corpus, preserve document order, and describe how the denominator was defined.

A useful quality check would sample entries from the beginning, middle, and end of every source PDF. A human reader could compare the extracted paragraph with the original page and record fragmented lines, repeated running headers, or joined bullet points. If these errors concentrate in a single party’s document layout, differences in the fitted topics may partly reflect extraction quality. A single corpus-wide error rate could obscure that imbalance.

Comparing Topic Diversity#

Fewer repeated words. Are the themes easier to name?

What Does a Diversity Score of 0.925 Measure?#

Consider an illustrative model with two topics and four top words per topic. There are eight positions in their top-word lists. If the two lists share one word and the other six positions contain distinct words, seven unique words occupy those eight positions. Topic diversity under this counting rule is seven divided by eight. The measure rewards lists that reuse less vocabulary across topics.

python
def topic_diversity(top_words):
    words = [word for topic in top_words for word in topic]
    if not words:
        raise ValueError("Supply at least one nonempty topic")
    return len(set(words)) / len(words)

example = [
    ["employment", "skills", "training", "wages"],
    ["health", "clinics", "staff", "training"],
]
print(topic_diversity(example))  # 7 unique words in 8 positions

The example illustrates the metric and uses invented word lists. For the reported comparison, the important feature is that both models were judged against the same corpus. To reproduce a diversity score one must also know how many topics were fitted, how many top words were retained per topic, and what happened to terms removed during preprocessing. Changing any of those choices can alter the amount of overlap even when the underlying documents stay fixed.

The observed difference is 0.262 on a scale from zero to one. That quantifies word reuse in the retained lists. It does not measure the fraction of paragraphs assigned to the correct policy area. There is no expert policy label in this evaluation that could support such a reading. Nor does it indicate whether every topic is useful: a single coherent but broad employment topic could be more informative to a policy reader than several distinct lists organised around recurring document language.

Why a Paragraph at a Time?#

A whole manifesto is too coarse a unit for the question. A party can discuss employment on one page and healthcare on the next. A paragraph gives the model a chance to distinguish those themes while keeping words close to the claims they qualify.

I extracted and cleaned the English text of the 2024 manifestos of BJP, Congress, CPI(M), and TMC. I built both a full dataset and a balanced one. The comparison here uses 208 paragraphs per party. That gives each party equal weight by paragraph count, although paragraph lengths and writing styles can still differ.

What Made the Comparison Informative?#

I used keyword rules to assign broad policy labels, including employment, education, health, and welfare.1 I also counted sentiment words separately. Those counts describe the vocabulary captured by the word lists.

For the topic models, I examined three different properties:

Measure Question it helps answer
Diversity Do several topics keep returning the same top words?
Coherence (NPMI) Do the words grouped into a topic tend to occur together?
Stability How consistently does the model recover its topics across runs?

What the Two Models Represent#

Latent Dirichlet allocation represents a document as a mixture of topics and each topic as a distribution over words. In this setting a paragraph can draw partly on education vocabulary and partly on employment vocabulary. The model assigns weights across these topics. Giving the paragraph a single label afterwards discards some of the information that motivated a mixture model in the first place. Blei, Ng and Jordan, 2003

Non-negative matrix factorisation decomposes a nonnegative document-by-term matrix into two smaller factors. One factor describes how strongly each paragraph draws on each component. The other supplies the terms associated with that component. The factors reconstruct the input through additive combinations. A useful way to inspect one component is to read its high-weight terms alongside the paragraphs on which it has large weights. Term lists alone can make an accidental association look more convincing than it is.

Neither method supplies policy names automatically. A label such as employment is an interpretation applied after inspecting the output. That interpretive step is particularly important where one theme crosses several policy areas. A paragraph promising training places can discuss education, labour markets, and an administrative programme in the same passage. A plausible high-weight topic can capture one aspect of that passage and still omit what a subject-matter reader considers most consequential.

Coherence and Stability Ask Different Questions#

A topic can have an unusual vocabulary because it captured a set of rare extraction errors. Such a component may boost diversity by contributing words that appear nowhere else. Coherence adds a check on whether its leading terms actually occur in related contexts. For normalised pointwise mutual information, pairs of words receive credit when their joint occurrence is stronger than would be expected from their marginal frequencies. The result depends on the reference corpus and the context window used to count occurrences.

Stability concerns repetition of the fit. Two runs may recover similar subjects while assigning them different numerical indices. A comparison that treats topic 1 in one run as the automatic counterpart of topic 1 in another could report instability merely from a change in ordering. For a replication I would first match topics on a documented similarity measure, then report which topics were consistently recovered and which ones lacked a close counterpart. A mean stability score can otherwise conceal one highly variable theme.

The three measures together support a more useful reading than any single ranking. High diversity and low coherence would suggest distinct lists of questionable interpretability. High coherence and low diversity could indicate several overlapping ways of describing one subject. Strong results on both, followed by poor stability, would call for caution when naming a theme from one particular fit. In this corpus the project report favoured NMF across all three measures. Interpretation and Limitations#

NMF produced more diverse, coherent, and stable topics in this comparison. The figure shows the diversity measure alone. Even a topic with a distinctive vocabulary can group paragraphs that a reader would assign to different policy areas.

My next check would be a small manual audit. I would sample paragraphs from each model’s strongest topics, hide the model identity, and ask whether a reader could give each group a consistent policy label. That would test an interpretation the diversity score leaves open.

Separating Topics, Policy Labels, and Sentiment#

The pipeline contains three different views of a paragraph. Keyword rules identify mentions from a predefined policy vocabulary. Lexicon counts indicate occurrences of words designated positive or negative. Topic weights describe recurring patterns discovered by a fitted model. These outputs can disagree without any implementation error. A paragraph about unemployment may use negative vocabulary to describe current conditions, promise future training, and receive high weight on an employment topic. A negative lexicon score in that paragraph is particularly easy to misread as a negative position on employment policy.

For a closer audit I would sample paragraphs with high topic weights and few matching policy keywords, and paragraphs with many keywords but weak weight on the corresponding topic. The first group can expose words the dictionary misses. The second can reveal boilerplate or general campaign language that triggers broad terms without sustained discussion of the policy. Both groups would be read in context, with the original document available to resolve references that a paragraph extraction leaves implicit.

I would also inspect the party composition of a topic’s highest-weight paragraphs. A concentration in one party is a lead for closer reading. It could indicate a distinctive policy emphasis, a repeated slogan, or a recurring heading specific to that PDF. Removing a repeated phrase and refitting on the training corpus would test one of these explanations. A substantive interpretation would need to survive such a change and remain supported by the underlying passages.

Conclusion and Further Validation#

The project established a reproducible path from four manifesto PDFs to paragraph-level comparisons and interpretable topic outputs. NMF produced a larger fraction of unique top words than LDA on the balanced corpus. Coherence and stability supplied complementary checks. The finding supports using NMF as the starting point for closer inspection of this collection. It does not establish that the same method will recover the most useful topics in another language, another election, or a corpus with a different paragraph structure.

My next evaluation would give two readers the same sampled passages independently and ask them to assign a policy label, mark overlapping topics, and flag genuinely ambiguous cases. I would record disagreement before resolving it. If readers disagree frequently even with access to context, that is evidence about the difficulty of the proposed target. If they agree but the model groups the passages differently, it is evidence about the representation or the fitting procedure. Those two outcomes imply different next steps.

A second sensitivity check would rerun the comparison on several balanced samples drawn from the full collection and inspect changes in both numeric rankings and individual themes. A method could win the average comparison while recovering a specific policy theme only occasionally. For a reader interested in that issue, recoverability of the individual theme is the relevant result. This check would keep the project’s strongest feature, an explicit comparison on shared data, while moving the evaluation closer to the purpose of reading manifestos in the first place.
